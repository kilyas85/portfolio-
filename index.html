Background

I'm an RF, electromagnetics, and electronics engineer with a multidisciplinary background spanning high-frequency electromagnetic simulation, mm-wave systems, analog CMOS design, and applied machine learning. My work focuses on combining physics-based engineering methods with computational and AI/ML approaches to solve complex problems efficiently and accurately.

My industrial R&D experience at NXP Semiconductors, through the EU-funded INNOSTAR consortium project focused on 5G and 6G connectivity, involved developing an AI/ML-assisted workflow to predict far-field radiation patterns from near-field IC data. The project covered the full engineering lifecycle, including problem definition, simulation methodology, dataset generation, deep-learning model development, verification and validation, and technical reporting within an international research consortium.

My technical foundation spans the main professional EM simulation environments — ANSYS HFSS, Keysight ADS, and CST Studio Suite — used across FEM and Method of Moments solvers. I've developed deep-learning architectures (CNN, U-Net) in PyTorch and built the data preprocessing pipelines that make them trainable from simulation outputs. Before that, I spent two years on integrated photonics research, designing dispersion-engineered waveguides published in international journals.

I'm currently based in Eindhoven, with technical interests spanning RF and electromagnetics, mm-wave systems, EMC, electronics and semiconductor technologies, EM simulation, and AI/ML for engineering applications.

Project 01 · NXP Semiconductors / TU/e
AI/ML Pipeline for mm-Wave EMC Prediction
Replacing full-wave simulation with deep learning for near-field to far-field mapping in RFIC EMC workflows
Deep Learning EMC PyTorch HFSS 5G/6G 100 GHz
▾
Context

Within the EU-funded INNOSTAR consortium — a European initiative for 5G and 6G semiconductor connectivity and radar applications — NXP Semiconductors needed to address a measurement gap in their mm-wave RFIC electromagnetic compliance process. Near-field (NF) data from integrated circuits was available; corresponding far-field (FF) radiation data for EMC verification was not directly accessible without costly full-wave simulation runs.

Challenge

Full-wave electromagnetic simulation at 100 GHz can be computationally intensive and time-consuming, particularly when evaluating many IC geometry variants. The project explored an AI/ML-assisted methodology for predicting far-field radiation patterns from near-field inputs, with the goal of reducing repeated simulation effort during design iterations.

My Role

I owned the technical workflow from research scope and simulation methodology through dataset generation, AI/ML development and validation, consortium reporting, and preparation of an NXP-approved journal manuscript.

Approach
  • Established a validated EM simulation methodology as the foundation for automated dataset generation and subsequent AI/ML development
  • Designed a parametric DOE study (ANSYS optiSLang) on two-layer stackup geometries to generate a diverse training dataset
  • Built Python/NumPy preprocessing pipelines to structure NF/FF simulation outputs into clean ML input-output pairs
  • Developed and compared CNN and U-Net architectures in PyTorch, evaluated across multiple loss functions
  • Performed systematic V&V by benchmarking model predictions against full-wave HFSS references
Tools & Skills
ANSYS HFSS (FEM) Keysight ADS (FEM/MoM) ANSYS optiSLang PyTorch CNN / U-Net Python / NumPy HPC / Linux DOE V&V Methodology
Skills Demonstrated
  • End-to-end R&D project ownership in an industrial consortium
  • AI/ML pipeline development for electromagnetic field prediction
  • Automated EM dataset generation and ML-ready data preparation
  • EU consortium reporting and formal deliverable preparation
  • Communication of novel computational workflows to industrial and academic stakeholders
Outcome & Impact

Delivered a working AI/ML pipeline for predicting far-field radiation from near-field inputs, aimed at reducing the need for repeated full-wave EM simulations during design studies. The pipeline was systematically validated against HFSS reference data, and the work resulted in an NXP-approved journal manuscript currently in preparation for submission.

Project 02 · NXP Semiconductors / TU/e
Cross-Solver Benchmarking at mm-Wave Frequencies
Systematic validation of FEM vs. MoM solvers for mm-wave RFIC radiated emission analysis across 10–100 GHz
HFSS ADS FEM/MoM Validation 10-100 GHz
▾
Context

Accurate electromagnetic simulation at mm-wave frequencies is critical for RFIC design and EMC compliance, but different simulation tools use different numerical methods (FEM and Method of Moments) that can produce divergent results if not properly understood. Before building ML models trained on simulation data, the reliability of that data had to be established.

Challenge

At 100 GHz, small geometric differences and solver-specific numerical choices can produce meaningfully different near-field and far-field results. Without systematic benchmarking, simulation data used to train ML models may carry solver-specific biases rather than physical ground truth.

Approach
  • Selected representative mm-wave small radiating structures as test vehicles
  • Simulated NF and FF radiated emission in ANSYS HFSS (FEM) and Keysight ADS (FEM and MoM) across 10–100 GHz
  • Systematically compared radiation patterns, S-parameters, and near-field distributions across both solvers
  • Assessed solver consistency and identified conditions where FEM and MoM results diverge
  • Documented findings to inform subsequent ML training data selection
Skills Demonstrated
  • Advanced proficiency in ANSYS HFSS and Keysight ADS as both FEM and MoM environments
  • Rigorous V&V methodology applied to simulation outputs
  • Understanding of numerical method trade-offs in high-frequency EM simulation
  • Systematic scientific documentation and reporting
Outcome

Established a validated simulation baseline that confirmed solver consistency conditions for the subsequent ML training data generation pipeline. The benchmarking methodology itself represents reusable validation infrastructure for future mm-wave RFIC EM analysis at NXP and beyond.

Project 04 · Quaid-i-Azam University
Dispersion-Engineered Photonic Waveguides for Supercontinuum Generation
Designing mid-IR and far-IR broadband light sources using integrated lithium niobate and silicon-rich nitride waveguides
Integrated Photonics COMSOL Nonlinear Optics Published Research
▾
Context

Broadband supercontinuum light sources in the mid- and far-infrared are critical for molecular spectroscopy, medical diagnostics, and environmental sensing. Photonic integrated waveguides offer a compact, chip-scale approach — but achieving broad spectral coverage requires precise engineering of the waveguide's dispersion profile.

Challenge

Designing waveguide geometries that simultaneously support anomalous dispersion (necessary for soliton-based supercontinuum generation), low propagation loss, and broad spectral coverage across multiple octaves. The design space is highly nonlinear and not analytically tractable — requiring systematic numerical optimization.

Approach
  • Modelled EM field distributions and dispersion profiles using COMSOL Multiphysics finite element simulation
  • Designed dual-core and Z-cut geometries in lithium niobate and silicon-rich nitride to engineer group velocity dispersion
  • Simulated nonlinear pulse propagation and supercontinuum generation dynamics in MATLAB using the generalized nonlinear Schrödinger equation
  • Achieved three-octave spectral coverage and tunable dispersive wave generation in the mid-IR and far-IR
Skills Demonstrated
  • Photonic waveguide design and COMSOL-based EM simulation
  • Nonlinear optical modelling and pulse propagation simulation
  • Scientific writing and international journal publication
  • Independent research from conception to published outcome
Outcome

Designed photonic waveguides achieving three-octave supercontinuum spanning and tunable dispersive wave generation across mid- and far-IR regions. Results published in the Journal of Electromagnetic Waves and Applications (2024), Optik (2022), and presented at the Optica Advanced Photonics Congress (2022).
